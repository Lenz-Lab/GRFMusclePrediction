About

What are we doing?

You can't stick a sensor directly on a muscle and ask it how hard it's pulling. Biomechanics research labs do the next best thing: track how the body moves using markered motion capture with sub-millimeter precision and sensors that measure how hard the foot hits the ground. The data is then worked 'backwards' through a musculoskeletal simulation (OpenSim) to estimate everything in between: joint moments via inverse dynamics, then individual muscle forces using static optimization.

The final step of that process is the most challenging. A single leg has 30+ muscles crossing the hip, knee, and ankle, but those joints only have a handful of degrees of freedom between them. There are infinitely more muscles than equations. Any given joint moment can be produced by countless different muscle-coordination strategies. Solving it requires an optimizer that typically functions by minimizing total muscle effort.

The motivation behind this project is that motion capture labs are expensive in money and time. Ground reaction forces are comparatively cheap and increasingly everywhere: force plates, instrumented walkways, and even insoles are seeing research and commercial use. If a model can learn the mapping from force plate input alone to what the full mocap-plus-OpenSim pipeline would have said, you get non-invasive muscle-force estimates without the lab.
